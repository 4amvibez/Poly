How do Japanese people read such a long sentence, without spaces?

In English, spaces tell us where one word ends, and the next begins. But Japanese? Words often run together, without spaces. So how do readers know where one word ends, and another begins?

The first clue is kanji. Look at this sentence.
私は日本語を勉強します。
Kanji carries important meaning. When readers see "nihongo", they can recognize the word for Japanese language.

Next comes hiragana. Characters like "wa", "o", and "shimasu" help readers understand how the sentence is structured. They show grammatical relationships, and how words work together.

And katakana? It often helps readers recognize foreign-origin words, names, and certain emphasized expressions. For example, "kohee" means coffee.

But here's the real secret. Japanese readers don't rely on just one clue. They combine kanji, kana, grammar, vocabulary, and context, to recognize meaningful chunks of a sentence. Their brains learn to see the boundaries, even when spaces aren't written.

At first, Japanese can look like one endless line. But once you understand the patterns, the sentence starts to make sense.
