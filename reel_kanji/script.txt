Did you know that some Japanese kanji started as pictures?

Thousands of years ago, people needed a way to record the world around them.

In ancient China, people carved symbols onto oracle bones and turtle shells. Some of these early characters represented things they could see in everyday life.

The sun. The moon. Mountains. Trees.

Look at this character: "hi", meaning sun.

Early forms resembled a drawing of the sun, often with a mark inside. Over centuries, its shape changed through different writing styles, until it became the character we recognize today.

And the idea doesn't stop there.

"Yama" means mountain. "Kawa" means river. "Ki" means tree.

Look closely, and you can see how some of these characters resemble the things they represent.

But here's the fascinating part: not every kanji is a picture.

Many characters combine a component related to meaning, with another that gives a clue to pronunciation.

This allowed the writing system to express far more than simple objects.

Over time, Chinese characters developed into a sophisticated writing system. They later reached Japan through historical cultural contacts, and were adapted to write the Japanese language.

These characters became known in Japanese as kanji.

So the next time you write a kanji, remember: behind that single character may lie centuries of history.
