Is kanji really necessary?
What if Japanese used only hiragana and katakana?

Look at this sentence:
母は花が好き。
It means, "Mother likes flowers."
Now, let's remove the kanji, and write everything in hiragana:
ははははながすき。
Wait... why does this suddenly look so confusing?

Japanese has many words that sound alike. Without kanji, readers may need more context to figure out which words and meanings are intended.
Kanji gives your eyes an important clue, helping you recognize meaningful words more easily.

Here's another example:
にわにはにわにわとりがいる。
Now look at it with kanji:
庭には二羽鶏がいる。
"There are two chickens in the garden."
See how the kanji helps make the meaning clearer?

So, is kanji really necessary?
Japanese can be written without kanji in some situations. But kanji helps make many everyday sentences easier to distinguish and read.
Hiragana, katakana, and kanji each have their own important role.

So, don't look at kanji as just another obstacle to overcome. Every character you learn helps you understand a little more of the Japanese language.
Don't fear kanji. Learn to see the meaning behind it.
